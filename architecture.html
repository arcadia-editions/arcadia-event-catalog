Catalog Inventory

Description

Catalog Inventory is the bounded context that owns scarce stock in Arcadia Editions. It protects edition availability by deciding whether a customer can claim a copy, holding that copy during checkout, and releasing it when the order does not complete.

It also supports backoffice inventory operations by tracking how much stock is available, reserved, sold, received, or adjusted for each SKU.

Scope

  • Reserve stock for an order during checkout
  • Release reserved stock when payment or scheduling fails
  • Track reservation lifecycle and release reasons
  • Maintain inventory positions for available, reserved, and sold quantities
  • Support stock intake and manual stock adjustments in backoffice flows

Main domain elements

  • `StockReservation`: aggregate for scarce inventory claims made during checkout
  • `InventoryPosition`: aggregate for backoffice inventory balances per SKU
  • `InventoryService`: reservation API and event-driven release handling
  • `InventoryBackofficeService`: stock intake and adjustment operations

Catalog Inventory

Overview

Catalog Inventory is the stewardship capability for the scarce, physical side of Arcadia Editions releases. It maintains a reliable business view of the copies associated with an offering and protects the integrity of limited-edition availability. Its purpose is to ensure that stock is represented thoughtfully throughout its operational life, from being brought into the estate to being set aside, released, sold, or corrected.

This page explains the enduring business role of Catalog Inventory. It is a publication-oriented description, not a substitute for the repository's formal artifacts. Those artifacts provide the detailed information needed by teams working with the service, while this page focuses on the language, scope, and value of the capability itself.

Domain context

Catalog Inventory belongs to the Inventory Management subdomain of the Catalog & Inventory domain. Inventory Management addresses the controlled availability of editions whose quantity matters to the business and to collectors. In this context, each unit carries commercial significance: availability must be represented with care so that the catalogue remains credible and the promise of a limited release is respected.

The subdomain distinguishes the commercial definition of a release from the operational position of its copies. Catalog Inventory concentrates on the latter. It provides the disciplined view required to understand whether units are available for use, temporarily committed, no longer available, or in need of backoffice attention. This creates a stable vocabulary for inventory decisions without conflating them with the wider presentation of the catalogue.

Responsibilities

Catalog Inventory maintains inventory positions for catalogue items and manages the lifecycle of scarce stock claims. It records the business effects of stock being received, adjusted, reserved, released, and sold so that the current position is meaningful and traceable. It also supports the careful handling of temporary commitments, ensuring that a copy can be held when appropriate and made available again when that commitment no longer applies.

The service supports backoffice stewardship as well as controlled allocation. Inventory teams can use its concepts to reflect stock intake and corrections, keeping the recorded position aligned with the physical reality of an edition. Its responsibilities are designed around consistency: an edition's scarcity should be understood from a single authoritative operational perspective.

Boundaries

Catalog Inventory owns stock positions and the business rules around reserving and releasing scarce copies. It does not define the commercial identity, editorial description, pricing, or publication status of a product. Nor does it own payment decisions, customer communications, transport activity, or the physical warehouse processes used to carry out a stock movement.

The service is also not a general asset register. Its boundary is the inventory meaning required for catalogue releases: quantities, commitments, and the operational status of available copies. Keeping this focus prevents accounting of scarcity from being diluted by concerns that belong to other business capabilities.

Business value

Catalog Inventory protects confidence in Arcadia Editions' limited releases. A clear and authoritative view of stock helps the organisation uphold the value of scarcity, make responsible commitments, and avoid presenting availability that cannot be honoured. It gives backoffice teams a dependable basis for managing exceptions and maintaining an accurate picture of the estate.

By separating inventory stewardship from catalogue definition, the service also makes change easier to reason about. Teams can improve stock practices without altering the commercial story of a release, while catalogue work can remain focused on the offer itself. The result is a more resilient operational foundation for a curated, limited-edition business. 2026-05-21

Catalog Products

Overview

Catalog Products is the publishing home for the commercial definition of every Arcadia Editions release. It gives each offering a clear, enduring identity and establishes the information needed for people across the business to understand what is being presented for sale. The service treats a product as more than a listing: it is the considered expression of an edition's title, character, commercial proposition, and place in the catalogue.

This page describes the business capability represented by Catalog Products. It is intended as a durable guide to the service's purpose and language, rather than a technical reference. The detailed contracts and artifacts in the repository remain the source for precise integration and delivery information.

Domain context

Catalog Products sits within the Catalog Management subdomain of the Catalog & Inventory domain. Catalog Management is concerned with defining the offer that Arcadia Editions brings to market: the releases it curates, the qualities that distinguish them, and the conditions under which they are presented as part of the active catalogue.

For a limited-edition business, a product definition provides the stable commercial frame around an edition. It captures the qualities that make a release recognisable and marketable, while allowing the catalogue to remain intentional as collections, seasons, and editorial priorities change. This bounded context creates a common business understanding of each release without turning the catalogue into a record of operational activity.

Responsibilities

Catalog Products maintains the authoritative commercial definition of a product. Its responsibilities include establishing product records, curating the customer-facing information that explains an offering, and keeping that information coherent throughout the product's catalogue life.

It manages the progression from an internal product definition to an offering that is suitable for publication, and later to a state in which it is no longer presented as active. This includes stewardship of descriptive, pricing, edition, and release-oriented information so that product material remains meaningful, accurate, and consistent over time.

The service also records the inventory-tracking intent associated with a release. That role is deliberately descriptive: it helps express how an offering should be understood in the catalogue without taking ownership of the day-to-day accounting of units. In this way, Catalog Products preserves the business meaning of an edition while leaving operational concerns in their proper place.

Boundaries

Catalog Products owns the commercial identity and publication lifecycle of a release. It does not determine the physical availability of copies, make allocation decisions, maintain fulfilment activity, or carry out financial processing. It is also not a content-management system for every possible editorial asset; its focus is the product information needed to define and present an offer coherently.

The boundary keeps catalogue decisions understandable and auditable. Changes to how a product is named, described, positioned, priced, or presented belong here when they affect the commercial definition. Matters concerned with operational execution, customer interaction, or downstream administration remain outside this service's responsibility.

Business value

By giving every release a dependable commercial definition, Catalog Products helps Arcadia Editions present a catalogue that is clear, curated, and aligned with its editorial standards. It reduces ambiguity around what an offering is and how it should be represented, supporting confident decisions by commercial and editorial teams.

The service also makes change more deliberate. A defined publication lifecycle allows the organisation to prepare releases thoughtfully, present them at the right moment, and retire them without losing the historical meaning of the catalogue. The result is a more trustworthy product estate: one that can evolve with the collection while keeping each edition's commercial story intact. 2026-05-21

Fulfillment Shipping

Overview

Fulfillment Shipping provides the operational shipping perspective within Arcadia Editions. Its purpose is to turn a commercially ready order into work that can be handled reliably by fulfillment operations. The service records and governs the business state needed to prepare a shipment, allowing that work to progress in a clear and auditable way from initial scheduling through delivery readiness or an operational exception.

This catalog page explains the business meaning of the service's published outcomes. It is intended for product, operations, and delivery teams who need a durable understanding of what Fulfillment Shipping represents. The detailed contracts, schemas, and integration artifacts remain the source of truth for technical implementation; this page focuses on the stable business language that gives those artifacts context.

Fulfillment Shipping treats shipping as an operational commitment rather than a simple status label. It makes the preparation of a physical delivery visible as a managed business capability, so that fulfillment activity can be planned, assessed, and followed consistently. The service supports a shared understanding of whether shipping work has been accepted, whether it can move forward, and whether it needs attention.

Domain context

The Fulfillment domain covers the operational handling that follows a completed commercial decision. Within that domain, Shipping is concerned with the preparation and readiness of goods for physical movement. Its focus is practical execution: ensuring that shipping work has enough business context to be organized, assessed, and represented as a meaningful operational outcome.

Shipping readiness is not assumed to be automatic. It depends on the ability to organize fulfillment work and to determine whether the shipment can proceed under the service's business rules. By expressing that assessment as part of the domain, Fulfillment Shipping creates a dependable record of operational progress without conflating it with commercial, payment, or customer-communication concerns.

Responsibilities

Fulfillment Shipping owns the business representation of shipment preparation. It establishes when fulfillment work has been scheduled, maintains the information required for that work, and evaluates whether the shipment is ready to continue. It also represents circumstances in which preparation cannot proceed as expected, preserving a clear operational outcome rather than leaving work in an ambiguous state.

The service provides a consistent lifecycle for shipping work. That lifecycle supports planning and follow-up by distinguishing work that is newly scheduled from work that is ready for the next operational stage, while making failure conditions explicit. These outcomes offer a stable vocabulary for people and systems that need to understand fulfillment progress at a business level.

Boundaries

Fulfillment Shipping is bounded by shipment scheduling and readiness. It does not own the commercial definition of an order, the financial treatment of a purchase, the maintenance of product information, or the delivery of customer communications. It also does not prescribe warehouse procedures, carrier-specific processes, route optimization, or the technical mechanics used to exchange information.

Its responsibility begins when shipping work needs to be represented and ends with a business-level readiness or failure outcome. Detailed operational execution may evolve over time, but the service maintains the durable distinction between work that can proceed and work that requires resolution. This boundary helps keep the fulfillment model focused, comprehensible, and adaptable.

Business value

By making shipment preparation explicit, Fulfillment Shipping gives operations a dependable way to understand progress toward physical delivery. Teams can reason about shipping work through shared business outcomes instead of inferring its state from scattered operational activity. This improves visibility, supports timely exception handling, and makes fulfillment performance easier to discuss in a consistent language. 2026-05-21

Payments Processing

Overview

Payments Processing describes the business facts that explain how a payment moves through its commercial lifecycle. It is the Arcadia Editions bounded context that establishes, manages, completes, or compensates a financial commitment associated with a purchase. This catalog provides a durable explanation of meaningful payment outcomes that business and technical audiences can use without relying on provider-specific or implementation-specific knowledge.

Payments are consequential business records. This context turns those changes into clear, interpretable outcomes. Its purpose is not to describe payment mechanics, but to make the business significance of a payment lifecycle visible and dependable.

Domain context

The Payments domain governs the financial side of a purchase. Its Payment Processing subdomain owns the lifecycle of a payment from the point it is considered for authorization through completion or reversal. It is the authoritative place for describing the status of a payment commitment and the business outcomes that alter that status.

A payment is a lifecycle entity, not a single moment. It can progress through stages that reflect increasing commercial certainty, or reach outcomes that prevent or undo that progression. The context makes these transitions understandable through a stable vocabulary. Readers can reason about whether a payment is ready to proceed, needs another attempt, has been completed, or has been compensated, without conflating those meanings.

Responsibilities

The context owns continuity of a payment across its lifecycle. A payment remains recognizable as the same commercial commitment while its status changes, enabling teams to understand its history and current significance. It records whether the commitment is authorized, whether an attempt has not succeeded, whether funds have been captured, and whether a previous state has been compensated. Keeping those details separate makes the catalog useful to a broad audience and protects its value as a long-lived description of payment outcomes.

Business value

Clear payment outcomes enable Arcadia Editions to treat financial activity as an understandable and auditable part of the customer experience. Teams can distinguish decisions, failures, completions, and compensations with confidence, improving communication with customers and supporting sound operational and financial reporting. The lifecycle perspective also reduces ambiguity when investigating payment status or measuring business performance.

This catalog is a stable reference that can outlast changes in vendors, delivery mechanisms, and internal implementation. v0.0.0
place-order-flow

Place Order Flow * * Covers the full lifecycle of a customer order during a limited edition drop, * from the moment the customer submits their intent through payment capture and * shipping confirmation — or cancellation if any step fails. * * Stock reservation happens before payment. In a scarcity-driven drop, inventory * is the binding constraint. We hold the item first and charge second. * * Payment runs in two phases. Authorization at checkout places a hold on the * customer's funds without moving money. Capture happens later, at fulfillment * time, when the order is physically ready to ship. If fulfillment fails after * authorization but before capture, the hold is voided — no charge ever appears * on the customer's statement. * * Transient payment errors are retried within the reservation window. A hard * decline from the processor is not retried — it goes straight to cancellation. * * Reservation expiry is owned by the Scheduler, not the payment service. If * payment is not resolved within ten minutes of the order being created, the * Scheduler fires ReservationExpired and the stock is returned to the pool.

    Stock is reserved immediately on receipt of the customer's request. * In a limited edition drop, inventory sells out in seconds. Holding the * item now, before payment, is what guarantees the customer gets what they * came for. If stock is gone, we tell the customer immediately and stop here.

Documentation

Place Order Flow *

  • Covers the full lifecycle of a customer order during a limited edition drop,
  • from the moment the customer submits their intent through payment capture and
  • shipping confirmation — or cancellation if any step fails.
  • Stock reservation happens before payment. In a scarcity-driven drop, inventory
  • is the binding constraint. We hold the item first and charge second.
  • Payment runs in two phases. Authorization at checkout places a hold on the
  • customer's funds without moving money. Capture happens later, at fulfillment
  • time, when the order is physically ready to ship. If fulfillment fails after
  • authorization but before capture, the hold is voided — no charge ever appears
  • on the customer's statement.
  • Transient payment errors are retried within the reservation window. A hard
  • decline from the processor is not retried — it goes straight to cancellation.
  • Reservation expiry is owned by the Scheduler, not the payment service. If
  • payment is not resolved within ten minutes of the order being created, the
  • Scheduler fires ReservationExpired and the stock is returned to the pool.
